The six sessions 🧭 · from a bill that tripled to a signed serving plan

The running case is Kiln, a constructed B2B software company. Its in-app assistant launched well, and then in one quarter the model bill tripled and the slowest users waited twice as long for the first word. One large API model answers every request. Maya, its platform engineer, has to make it cheaper and faster without making it worse. Kiln and everyone in it are invented for teaching, and every page says so; the prompts on the bench are real.

What is honest here 🧾

Serving travels with big multipliers, and each one is true on its own benchmark. Each claim on these pages carries its status.

ClaimStatus
Router quality, routing share and cost per 1,000 requests on the benchReally computed in your browser from 2,000 real prompts released with the RouteLLM paper (Apache-2.0), with a judge's score of the small model's real answer, and checked against an independent Python run
What a GPU serves at a first-token targetModelled from spec-sheet bandwidth and compute plus a seeded queue, labelled on every number, and optimistic against vLLM's own reported speed
API, cache and GPU-hour pricesVendor list prices read on 2026-11-08; they change often, so re-verify
"Routers cut cost by 2x, or 85%, or 98%"Each on its own benchmark (RouteLLM paper, its README, FrugalGPT); session 2 sets them beside a floor on real rows
"FP8 is lossless, INT4 loses quality"One large study (Kurtic et al.) finds FP8 effectively lossless and INT4 weight-only close to 8-bit on its evals; session 4 says test your own task
"Engine X is 2x to 36x faster"Each against its own baseline of the day; the multipliers do not stack
